Why Most Apps Fail
(And What We Do Different)

A lot of software gets installed and then ignored. The usual reason is that the builder started with a feature they wanted to make, and went looking for someone who might need it.

Open your phone. Count the icons you have not touched this month. That pile is the problem this site is trying not to add to.

This page does not quote a churn study. The pattern is visible without one: a tool survives when it finishes a job the person already had, and it disappears when it asks them to learn a new job first.

The "Cool Feature" Trap

Most apps are built backwards. A developer thinks "wouldn't it be cool if..." and starts building. The problem comes second — or worse, gets retrofitted to justify the feature.

The pet-travel pages on this site are a good example of the cut. A search engine that “plans the whole trip” sounds larger. The question a person actually has, with a booking tab open, is narrower: what is the fee, what is the weight limit, and can the animal stay in the room. That is the page we published. The older PetWander host is not linked, because its certificate is expired. A dead app is not a feature.

The useful version of a tool is the one that answers the question in front of the person, and then stops.

What People Actually Want

The tools on this site follow the same constraints:

  • Speed beats features. People will use a fast, simple tool over a slow, powerful one every time.
  • Offline-first wins. If your app requires an internet connection to function, you've already lost half your potential users.
  • No login unless the tool cannot work without one. The calculators on this site do not have accounts.
  • One problem, solved completely. Better to nail one workflow than to half-solve ten.

The SoulstormAI Product Philosophy

Every app we build follows three rules:

1. Start with the problem, not the solution. We spend more time talking to users about their frustrations than we do coding. TipSplit exists because bill-splitting apps required account creation and had confusing UIs. The solution: instant calculator, no login, no confusion.

2. Ruthlessly cut features. Every feature is a liability. It's code to maintain, UI to explain, and complexity for users to navigate. If it doesn't directly solve the core problem, it doesn't ship.

3. Usable without an account. The calculators on this domain run in the browser. Saved deals stay on the device. A tool that cannot finish its job without a signup is a different product, and it is not what these pages are.

Case Study: House Flip Calculator

A flip can be wrapped in a property search, a contractor directory, and a map of “hot markets.” None of that tells you whether the house you are staring at works. The house flip calculator is the smaller thing: purchase, rehab, holding, selling, financing, and a 70% rule check on those inputs.

State presets are examples so the form is not blank. They are not a ranking of markets, and the page says so. Saved deals stay in the browser. There is no account and no “pro” tier on that page.

What "Better" Actually Means

Better doesn't mean more features. It means:

  • Finishes one job
  • Does not require an account on this site
  • Shows its assumptions instead of hiding them in a score
  • Doesn't try to upsell you
  • Doesn't track you more than necessary

That's the product philosophy. Build tools that respect people's time and intelligence. Don't add features for the sake of features. Solve one problem completely instead of ten problems poorly.

The Long-Term Play

A download count does not tell you whether the tool finished the job. A person who comes back because the worksheet matched the problem is the result worth having.

People don't uninstall apps that solve real problems. They don't churn from tools that save them time. They don't abandon software that actually works.
